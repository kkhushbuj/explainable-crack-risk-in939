What this does not show

Limits, stated up front.

The two decisions that shaped the method were reviewed and approved by the project supervisor, Prof. Gregory Gibbons.

  1. Small real sample

    Ten acoustic recordings are enough to read one at a time, not to train or statistically validate a model.

  2. Proxy labels, not confirmed ones

    Sensor readings are inferred from signal shape. No CT scan or sectioning confirms them.

  3. Unsynchronised recordings

    Acoustic data comes from tensile dogbones and thermal data from density cubes. They share no timeline, so they are analysed separately.

  4. Estimated print settings

    The real specimens’ laser power, speed, hatch and layer values were not recorded. A matching reference paper’s recipe stands in.

  5. Imbalanced literature

    Only 15 crack-free examples exist across all ten papers. That reflects the alloy, but it makes the minority-class results fragile.

  6. Validation is not grouped by paper

    Samples from one paper share a printer, powder and reporting style. Testing on a paper the model has never seen may score lower than 92.2%.

  7. Doping is tied to four papers

    All 13 doped samples come from four studies, so the doping result is partly confounded with the source.
